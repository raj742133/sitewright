One night on the ridge

From dusk to first light, slowly.From dusk to first light, slowly.

19:401199::4400Dusk

  1. The climb

    Three hundred stone steps, a lantern at every twentieth one, and a guide who walks last. Bring nothing you cannot carry in one hand.

  2. The bell is struck

    We carry paper lanterns to the upper terrace and set them on the water. The bell sounds once for every guest on the mountain.

  3. Silent supper

    Mushroom broth, pickles, rice from the valley below. We eat without talking, then a little tea, then a lot of stars.

  4. The ridge turns rose

    Sit on the east steps while the ridge goes from ink to rose. Coffee is optional, and so is waking up.

What the house does

Four small rituals

Four small habits, kept for centuries. None of them is a performance. They are how a mountain house learns to be quiet.Four small habits, kept for centuries. None of them is a performance. They are how a mountain house learns to be quiet.

  • 19:30

    Lantern walk

    A guided, unhurried loop of the upper terraces with paper lanterns made on site that afternoon.

    Runs nightly at dusk, about 70 minutes, suitable for most mobility levels with the shorter route.

  • 23:30

    Midnight tea

    Hand-roasted tea at the low table, poured in the order the house has always poured it.

    Served from 23:30 to 00:30. Decaffeinated roasted barley tea is always available.

  • 00:00

    The bell

    One slow strike at nine, midnight and first light. You hear it in your bones before your ears.

    Guests may ring the midnight bell once. We ask for one strike, no more.

  • 05:40

    Sunrise sit

    Twenty minutes of nothing, facing the east, with a blanket and a flask of something warm.

    Cushions and blankets are on the steps from 05:00. The sit ends when the first light touches the roof.

Letters from the ridge

One short note when the first frost arrives and when the lanterns return. No more than six a year.
