Method

Three rules for binding a tool.

  1. 01

    Bind it, then leave it alone

    Every tool ships as a finished volume. We add chapters, not features, and we never rewrite a page you already rely on.

  2. 02

    Small enough to read in an evening

    If the manual takes longer than a night to read, the tool is too big. We cut until it fits on one shelf.

  3. 03

    Printed to be reread

    Plain type, honest names, no dark corners. You should be able to pick a tool up after a year and know where everything is.

Journal

Notes from the bindery.

  • Plugins turn a tool into a platform, and platforms turn into maintenance. We would rather keep Kiln small enough to read in full, and give it three well-chosen hooks instead of a marketplace.

  • People log less time than they think and more than they fear. The most used feature is still the blank week: a calendar with nothing on it and permission to start late.

  • A beta is a manuscript. We put it on the shelf early, dog-eared and unfinished, so readers can write in the margins before the print run.
