How it works

Four steps, one conversation.

  1. 1

    Ask

    Claude asks which modules you want, then your brand name, two colours and what you call things: orders, cases, scans.

  2. 2

    Configure

    Your answers become one site.json. Skip anything and a sensible default fills in; nothing factual is invented.

  3. 3

    Generate

    A complete Next.js project: pages, API routes, database schema, storage, middleware, a README.

  4. 4

    Verify

    It builds the site and drives it in Chromium at 1280, 390 and 360 px, then shows what passed.

FAQ

Questions people ask.

Does the generated site depend on Sitewright?

No. You get a normal Next.js project in a folder of your choice. Delete the skill and the site keeps working.

Where does my data live?

In your own database and storage. In development it is an embedded Postgres in a folder inside the project plus local files. For production you point DATABASE_URL at Postgres and choose local, S3/R2 or Azure Blob storage.

Does it make up numbers or testimonials?

No. Sections that need facts (proof numbers, feature lists, compatibility) are off until you give real content. The demo screenshots here use clearly fictional data.

How secure is the sign-in?

It follows common practice: scrypt hashes, signed cookies, authenticator codes that cannot be reused, lockout after repeated failures. It has not had an independent security audit, so review it before putting anything sensitive behind it.

Where do the effects come from? Is this React Bits?

No. Those libraries’ licences do not allow redistributing them inside another package, so these effects are written fresh for this kit, need no animation library, and are MIT licensed. Nineteen of the line figures are Hairline by Lucas Marques, also MIT, included with its licence notice; three more are new here. Icons come from the open sets behind Iconify, each under its own licence, which the generated site records.

Can I change the design afterwards?

Yes. The colours are CSS variables at the top of globals.css, the logo is one file, and everything else is ordinary React you can edit.
